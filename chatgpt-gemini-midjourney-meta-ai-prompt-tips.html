Prompt tips for ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney and Meta AI

The same prompt rarely behaves the same way in two tools. Here is how to adapt your wording for ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney and Meta AI.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Many of our readers copy a prompt from PixPrompts and paste it into more than one AI tool. That is a good habit, because the results vary a lot between tools. This guide explains the general differences, how to adapt your prompt and what to keep in mind. Features change often, so always check the current abilities and limits of each tool before you begin.

Why one prompt gives different results everywhere

Every AI tool is trained differently and has its own way of reading instructions. One may follow every detail of a long prompt, while another focuses on the style and ignores the small print. Some tools can edit your uploaded photo directly, and some mainly create new images from text. So treat each prompt as a starting point and not a guarantee.

Two kinds of tools: editing and generating

Editing tools take your photo and change it according to your instruction. They are the best choice when you want to keep your own face and surroundings. Generating tools create a new picture from a description, and a photo you give them may serve only as a loose reference. Before using a prompt that says “keep my face unchanged”, check that your tool can really edit uploaded images. If it cannot, a generated picture will be inspired by you but will not be you.

ChatGPT

Conversational tools like ChatGPT are good at following long, natural instructions and at back-and-forth changes. You can upload a photo, describe the edit in plain sentences and then refine with follow-ups such as “keep the pose but make the light warmer”. Because it is a conversation, you can fix one thing at a time instead of rewriting everything.

Gemini

Gemini also works well with plain language and uploaded photos. Clear, short sentences tend to work best. Say what to change, then what to keep. It can be helpful to describe the result as a final image, for example “a realistic portrait of me at golden hour”, and add a separate line telling it to preserve your features.

Midjourney

Midjourney is known for beautiful, artistic images and is mostly built around generating pictures. It tends to prefer descriptive phrases about style, mood and lighting over full conversational sentences. It has its own settings and parameters for things like aspect ratio and image references, and these change over time, so read its current documentation. If your goal is a stylised or fantasy picture, it can be a good fit. If your goal is a faithful edit of your own face, test carefully and keep expectations realistic.

Meta AI

Meta AI is available inside apps many people already use, which makes it convenient for quick, casual edits and images. Keep prompts short and direct. Start with a simple request, look at the result and then add detail. Quick tests like this show you how closely the tool follows your instructions.

How to adapt a prompt between tools

  • Shorten it if the tool seems to ignore the ending. Put the most important instructions first.
  • Add structure if the tool seems confused. Use the order: style, light, setting, what must stay unchanged.
  • Swap sentences for phrases in tools that prefer descriptive keywords.
  • Test with the same photo in each tool so you can compare fairly.

A reliable template that travels well

Edit this photo into [style]. Use [light type and direction]. Set it in [simple background]. Keep my face, hairstyle and outfit unchanged. This short form is easy for almost any tool to understand, and you can add details after you see the first result.

Check each tool’s rules

Every provider has its own terms, content rules, age limits and privacy policy. Read them, especially what happens to photos you upload. Do not upload pictures of people without their permission, and never use edits to mislead others. PixPrompts is an independent prompt library and is not affiliated with any AI provider. Some features may need a paid plan, and we recommend checking the official pricing pages directly.

Keep your own notes

Write down which prompt worked best in which tool. After a few weeks you will know, for example, which tool is best for realistic portraits and which is best for artistic styles. That personal knowledge is more valuable than any list.
