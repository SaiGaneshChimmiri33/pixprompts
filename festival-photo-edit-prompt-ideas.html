Festival photo edit prompt ideas: Diwali, weddings and more

Ideas for Diwali, weddings, Pongal, Eid, Holi and more, with advice on keeping outfits, jewellery and faces looking true to life.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Festivals are when we take the most photos and when we most want them to look special. AI editing can add a glow of diyas, a marigold backdrop or a soft golden evening, but festive pictures are also tricky: sarees, kurtas, jewellery and faces all carry detail that matters to you. This guide gives you ideas for common occasions and explains how to keep the people in your photos looking like themselves.

Start with a concrete setting

The word “festive” alone gives generic results. A specific scene gives the AI something to build, so describe actual objects and places. Instead of “make this a Diwali photo”, try “soft evening light with rows of glowing diyas, hanging string lights and a rangoli on the floor”. The more concrete the setting, the more convincing the picture.

Ideas by occasion

Diwali

Think warm gold, deep orange and a night sky. Mention diyas, fairy lights, paper lanterns and a softly blurred background. Try: “warm Diwali evening, glowing diyas in the foreground, string lights behind, rich golden tones, face softly lit”.

Weddings and engagements

Marigold garlands, a floral mandap and soft cinematic light suit wedding pictures. Try: “marigold and jasmine decoration, soft backdrop, warm cinematic light, elegant and natural”. For couple photos, ask that both faces and outfits remain unchanged.

Eid and Ramadan

Soft crescent moon, lanterns and a calm evening sky create a peaceful mood. Try: “quiet twilight with hanging lanterns, a crescent moon in the sky, warm gentle light on the subject”. Keep the mood respectful and avoid altering prayer items or religious text.

Holi

Holi pictures are about colour and joy. Ask for “bright, joyful colour powder in the air, natural daylight, sharp face and clothes”. Because colours are strong, add “keep skin tones natural” so faces do not turn pink or blue.

Pongal, Sankranti and Ugadi

Harvest and new year festivals suit morning light, traditional kolam designs, sugarcane, mango leaf torans and earthy colours. Try: “soft morning sunlight, kolam at the doorway, traditional decor, warm natural colours”.

Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri and Durga Puja

These are colourful, crowded celebrations. For portraits, ask for a decorated pandal in soft focus behind you, with warm lights. Keep the request simple so the tool does not add extra faces or distort the idol.

Christmas and New Year

Fairy lights, a decorated tree and soft winter tones create a cosy scene. Try: “warm indoor light, twinkling tree lights in the background, soft red and gold tones”.

Protect traditional outfits and jewellery

Sarees, lehengas, kurtas and temple jewellery have fine borders, embroidery and patterns that AI often redraws. To keep them, say so clearly: “keep my outfit design, colours and jewellery exactly the same”. If you want richer colour, ask only for “slightly richer, natural outfit colours” rather than a different design. Check the zari border, the necklace and the bangles after each edit.

Keep faces accurate

Group and family photos are the hardest because there are more faces to preserve. Edit people one at a time when possible, or crop to one or two people. Include a line such as “keep every person’s face, hairstyle and skin tone unchanged”. Our guide on keeping your face accurate explains this in more depth.

Choose warm light carefully

Festive pictures look best with warm light, but too much orange hides natural skin colour. Ask for “warm golden light with natural skin tones”. For more lighting ideas, see our lighting words guide.

Keep it respectful

  • Edit only photos you own or have permission to use.
  • Do not alter religious symbols, idols or sacred text in a misleading or disrespectful way.
  • Do not create edited pictures that make it look as if someone attended an event they did not, if that could mislead people.
  • Ask family members before posting edited pictures of them online.

A festival prompt template

Use this pattern and fill in the brackets: [Festival setting with 2-3 concrete objects], [light type and direction], [mood], keep my face, outfit and jewellery unchanged. For example: “Diwali evening setting with glowing diyas and string lights, warm golden light from the front, joyful and elegant mood, keep my face, outfit and jewellery unchanged.”
