How to prepare your photo before AI editing

Ten minutes of prep before you upload saves you from most of the disappointing results people blame on the prompt.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

When an AI edit goes wrong, most of us rewrite the prompt again and again. In our experience the problem is usually the photo, not the words. The AI can only work with what it can see, so a few minutes spent preparing the picture often does more than an hour of prompt tweaking. This guide walks through what we check before every upload.

Start with the original file, not a copy

Every time a photo is forwarded on a messaging app, screenshotted or saved from social media, it gets compressed and loses fine detail. Eyes, eyelashes, fabric texture and the edge of your hair are the first things to go. When the AI later tries to “improve” the picture, it has to guess those missing details, and that is where faces start to drift.

So go back to the source. Use the file straight from your phone gallery or camera. If someone sent you the picture, ask them for the original instead of the version that came through chat. A photo that is a few megabytes is almost always a better starting point than one that is a few hundred kilobytes.

Check the light on the face

Lighting is the single biggest factor in how accurate an edit looks. Soft, even light lets the AI read the shape of your face clearly. Harsh shadows, a strong flash or a bright window behind you make it guess.

  • Good: daylight from a window beside you, open shade outdoors, or the soft hour after sunrise.
  • Risky: overhead tube lights, direct midday sun, a flash straight on the face, or a bright background that turns you into a silhouette.

If you are taking a fresh photo, simply turn your face towards the light source. It costs nothing and makes a noticeable difference.

Keep the face sharp and fully visible

Hair across the eyes, sunglasses, a hand covering the chin or a heavy mask all hide details the AI needs to preserve. If you can, choose a picture where both eyes are open, the face is turned mostly towards the camera and nothing important is cut off. Slight angles are fine. Extreme close-ups and photos taken from far away are harder, because either the features are distorted by the lens or they are only a few pixels wide.

Filters are another trap. Beauty filters and heavy colour grading change your skin and features before the AI even starts. If you want your real face kept, begin with an unfiltered photo.

Think about framing and space

If you plan to change the background, extend the picture or add a scene, leave some room around the subject. A photo where your head touches the top edge and your hands are cut at the wrist gives the AI nothing to build on, so it will invent awkward shapes. A little breathing space on all sides is ideal. As a rough guide, keep the subject in the middle two thirds of the frame.

Orientation matters too. For wallpapers and phone backgrounds use a vertical photo. For banners and thumbnails use a horizontal one. Cropping the picture to the shape you want before uploading is much more reliable than asking the AI to change the shape afterwards.

Clean up the background

Busy backgrounds with several people, signs or clutter confuse edits. The AI may blend a stranger’s arm into your outfit or copy a pattern onto your clothes. When you can, take the photo against a plain wall or an uncluttered outdoor spot. If it is too late for that, crop tightly so only the person you are editing remains.

Mind privacy before you hit upload

This step is easy to forget. Before uploading, look at everything visible in the frame.

  • Remove or crop out documents, ID cards, bills, screens and house numbers.
  • Do not upload photos of other people, and especially children, unless you have their permission or you are their guardian.
  • Read the privacy policy of the AI tool you are using, so you know how it treats uploaded pictures.

PixPrompts itself never receives your photo. You copy a prompt here and use it in the AI tool of your choice, so your pictures stay between you and that tool.

A quick pre-upload checklist

  • Is this the original file, not a screenshot or forwarded copy?
  • Is the face lit softly and evenly?
  • Are both eyes visible and the image sharp?
  • Is there room around the subject for the edit you want?
  • Is the background simple?
  • Is there nothing private in the frame, and do I have the right to edit this picture?

What to do if you only have a poor photo

Sometimes the only picture you have is old, small or blurry. That is fine, but set your expectations. Keep the edit modest, such as colour, mood or background, rather than asking for a big transformation. Add a line telling the AI to keep the face and features unchanged, and compare the result with the original. If the face has changed, try the edit again with a lower-key request or find a better source photo.
