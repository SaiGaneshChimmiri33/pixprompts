How to fix common AI photo editing mistakes

Plastic skin, strange hands, crunchy colours and messy edges: what causes each problem and what to change in your next attempt.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

AI photo editing can look magical, and it can also produce results that make you laugh or sigh. The good news is that the most common mistakes have fairly predictable causes, and most of them can be fixed by changing one thing at a time. Here are the problems we see most often and what we try first.

Plastic or over-smooth skin

Why it happens: words like “flawless”, “perfect” and “glowing” tell many tools to erase texture. What to try: add “natural skin texture, visible pores, minimal retouching” to the prompt and remove beauty words. If your tool has a strength or intensity setting, lower it. A slightly imperfect skin looks far more believable than a polished one.

The face no longer looks like you

Why it happens: a big style change, a low-quality source photo or both. What to try: start from a sharper, better-lit photo and add a sentence such as “keep my face, hairstyle and proportions identical to the original”. Reduce how much you are changing in one go. Our guide on keeping your face accurate goes deeper into this one.

Colours look too strong or fake

Why it happens: prompts packed with words like “vibrant”, “vivid” and “ultra colourful” make saturation unnatural. What to try: describe a mood instead, such as soft pastel, muted film tones or warm golden light. Naming a real-world reference, for example “like a 35mm film photo from the 1990s”, gives the tool a more natural target.

Messy hair edges and cut-out look

Why it happens: when the background is replaced, thin strands of hair and soft edges are hard to separate. What to try: ask for “clean, natural hair edges that blend with the new background” and choose a background with similar lighting and colour to the original. If the edges are still rough, do the edit in two steps: first change the background, then ask for a gentle blend or lighting match.

Strange hands and fingers

Why it happens: hands are complicated, and AI tools still get them wrong, especially when they are holding objects. What to try: choose a source photo where the hands are visible and simple, add “keep the hands and fingers exactly as in the original”, or crop the picture so hands are out of the frame. Re-running the edit a few times can also help, since each attempt differs.

Text, logos and jewellery get distorted

Why it happens: fine details such as lettering on a T-shirt, a necklace chain or an earring are tiny patterns that the tool may redraw. What to try: state that they must stay unchanged, for example “keep my necklace and earrings exactly the same”. For anything where the exact text matters, such as a name board or a poster, add the lettering back yourself in a basic editing app after the AI edit.

Lighting does not match

A person lit from the left on a background lit from the right looks pasted in. Mention the light direction and quality in your prompt, such as “soft window light from the left, matching shadows”. Our guide on lighting words has many phrases you can borrow.

Extra objects or people appear

Sometimes the tool adds things you never asked for, like a stray hand, a second face or random decorations. Be specific about what should be in the picture, and add “no extra people, no extra objects”. A simpler background request usually reduces this.

The picture is blurry or low resolution

Many tools return smaller images than the original. Start with the highest quality photo you have and, once you are happy with the edit, use an upscaling feature if your tool provides one. Avoid repeated edits on the same image, because quality can degrade a little each time.

A simple fixing routine

  • Look at the result next to the original and name the single biggest problem.
  • Change only the part of the prompt that relates to that problem.
  • Run it again and compare. Repeat once or twice.
  • If it still fails, try a different source photo or another AI tool.

The mistake most beginners make is rewriting the entire prompt every time. When you change one thing, you learn what actually worked, and you build a personal set of wording that you can reuse. To explore ready-made wording, browse our free prompts.
