How to write AI photo editing prompts that work

A simple, repeatable structure for writing prompts that give you the result you pictured, with examples you can adapt.

Last updated: November 22, 2026

Most poor AI results come from vague instructions. “Make it nice” and “make it cool” leave the tool to guess, and its guess is rarely your taste. The good news is that writing a strong prompt is a skill that anyone can learn in an afternoon. Below is the structure we use for almost every prompt on PixPrompts.

The five-part formula

A reliable prompt answers five questions: what style, what light, what setting, what mood and what must stay the same. You do not need long paragraphs. Five short phrases are often enough.

1. Start with the look

Name the style you want, such as cinematic, vintage film, studio headshot, watercolour, anime or magazine editorial. A clear style gives the tool a direction. Then add one or two supporting details, like “soft film grain” or “muted colours”. Avoid piling on ten styles, because the tool will average them out and the result looks confused.

2. Describe the light

Light changes the mood of a photo more than anything else. Golden hour gives warm, glowing edges. Window light shapes a face gently. Overcast light keeps colours natural. Neon adds drama at night. If your result looks flat or fake, a better lighting description is usually the fix. Our lighting words guide has a full list you can copy from.

3. Set the scene

Describe the place with two or three real objects: “a quiet cafe with wooden tables and a blurred window behind”, or “a rooftop at dusk with city lights”. Concrete details work much better than words like “beautiful background”. Keep it simple. A simple scene lets the subject stand out and reduces odd extra objects.

4. Add the mood

One or two feeling words help the tool match the atmosphere: calm, joyful, elegant, nostalgic, mysterious, warm. Be careful with words that push the tool to over-edit, such as “perfect” or “flawless”. If you want a natural look, say so directly: “natural skin texture, minimal retouching”.

5. Say what must stay the same

AI tools sometimes alter faces, clothing and objects while changing the style. End your prompt with a sentence that protects what matters, for example: “Keep my face, features, hairstyle and outfit unchanged.” The prompts on PixPrompts include a line like this because it makes edits more faithful. For more on this, read how to keep your face accurate.

A prompt built step by step

Weak: “Make my photo look cool.”

Better: “Make my photo look like a vintage film portrait.”

Strong: “Edit this photo into a vintage 35mm film portrait. Soft window light from the left, gentle shadows, natural skin texture. A quiet cafe with a blurred background. Calm, nostalgic mood, muted warm colours, subtle film grain. Keep my face, hairstyle and clothing unchanged.”

The strong version is only a few lines long, but every phrase does a job.

Change one thing at a time

When the first result is close but not right, do not rewrite everything. Change one detail. If the skin looks too smooth, ask for natural texture. If the colours are too strong, ask for muted tones. Small steps teach you which words really matter, while complete rewrites teach you nothing.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too vague: “make it good” gives the tool nothing to work with.
  • Too crowded: five styles, three lighting setups and four locations in one prompt cause confusion.
  • Contradictions: “bright sunny day” and “dark moody night” cannot both be true.
  • Forgetting to protect the subject: if the face matters, say so.
  • Ignoring the source photo: a blurry, dark picture will limit any prompt. See how to prepare your photo.

Adapt prompts for different tools

The same wording can behave differently in different AI tools. Some like full sentences, others prefer short descriptive phrases. We cover this in our guide on prompt tips for ChatGPT, Gemini, Midjourney and Meta AI.

Keep a prompt notebook

When a prompt works, save it. Reusing proven prompts on new photos gives you a consistent personal style, and you can combine pieces from different prompts, for example the lighting from one and the setting from another. After a month, you will have a toolbox that is entirely yours.
